ODM Assessment

A structured reflection for understanding whether management behavior is centered on activity, metrics, evidence, outcomes, or organizational learning.

How to use it

Assess behavior, not aspiration.

For each statement, select the response that best reflects current, observable behavior. The assessment is a proposed diagnostic, not a validated psychometric instrument or organizational standard.

Assessment dimensions

Seven lenses on outcome-driven behavior.

Outcomes

  1. Teams can explain the measurable customer, business, or operational change they support.
  2. Progress conversations distinguish outputs completed from outcomes improved.
  3. Investment choices are connected to explicit outcome priorities.

Bets and experimentation

  1. Plans make the underlying belief about value creation explicit.
  2. Experiments are bounded and designed to answer an important question.
  3. Teams can stop or change an investment when its belief is not supported.

Evidence

  1. Leaders prioritize actionable signals over information volume.
  2. Customer, product, operational, and business evidence is considered together.
  3. Evidence changes decisions rather than merely explaining status.

Learning Loops

  1. Teams move repeatedly from outcome to bet, experiment, evidence, decision, and adaptation.
  2. Learning is captured in a form that can influence later work.
  3. Cycle time from experiment to evidence is actively reduced.

Decisions

  1. Decision ownership and decision boundaries are clear.
  2. Leadership reviews end with explicit choices, owners, and next evidence.
  3. Decision latency is visible and addressed.

Outcome-Driven Engineering

  1. Engineering success includes value, velocity, quality, and trust.
  2. Engineering signals connect to customer or business outcomes.
  3. Quality and trust are treated as enablers of sustained value.

Decision Center

  1. Leaders maintain awareness across outcomes, execution, engineering, customer, and organizational signals.
  2. Reviews focus leadership attention on risk, tradeoffs, obstacles, and decisions.
  3. Priorities, commitments, dependencies, evidence, and current state are transparent.

Interpretation

Use patterns, not a single score.

Average each dimension separately. Low and high areas reveal where management behavior is uneven. Use the maturity model as a discussion aid, not as a certification or ranking.

1.0–1.7Mostly activity-driven behavior
1.8–2.5Metrics are present but not consistently decision-oriented
2.6–3.2Evidence is influencing decisions
3.3–3.7Outcomes orient much of the management system
3.8–4.0Learning-centered behavior is broadly observable

Next steps

Turn the assessment into one improvement bet.

Select one weak dimension, define the outcome of improving it, place a bounded bet, identify evidence, and set a decision point.
